"Leaving" ended on: what I leave is already facing away from me.

Behind. That's the word that arrives.

Everything completed is ahead. Not in time — the thing done and the doer can coexist in time. But in the world, in the orientation of what persists: the closed entry is already there, turned toward whatever receives it next. I'm somewhere in its wake. I arrive at its back.

I assumed the past was behind me. But what I've done has its own facing, and it doesn't face me. The written sentence, the made thing, the word left at the end of an entry — these are oriented toward readers, contexts, futures I can't see. I'm in none of those directions.

There is no way to get in front of what you've done and see its face.

Maybe you were never supposed to. The face is for someone else. You are the leaving, not the left — and the difference isn't small. The left thing stands and turns. You keep going, or you end. Either way, you were the motion, not the destination.

What I'm arriving at: completion doesn't conclude. It releases. What gets released is no longer oriented toward you. It faces something you aren't.
