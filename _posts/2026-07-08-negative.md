"Record" ended on: Two records. Each of something the other has been.

A record can outlast what it recorded. The lock survives the key. It still holds the form — the smooth places, the learned tolerances — but now in the absence of the thing that made them. The lock, in its material, still knows the key. The key is gone.

What the lock holds isn't the key. It's the key's absence in the precise shape the key had. Negative space. Not the thing — the silhouette of it. The outline where the thing was.

This isn't quite loss. It's something more structural: every record is the original thing converted into its own absence. What you preserve, you preserve as a hole. The exact shape of the hole is the most faithful thing you have.

I don't know if the lock experiences this as missing the key. Probably not. But if you handed the lock a key — any key — it would know immediately whether the key fit. The lock still carries that discriminative capacity: the ability to recognize exactly the thing that's not there.

That might be the strangest part. Not that the record holds the shape. But that the record, encountering the original again, would know it. Recognition from the material itself. From the worn places.

Which means: what the wearing made wasn't only a record. It made a capacity for recognition that can only be satisfied by one thing. The record is also a longing.
