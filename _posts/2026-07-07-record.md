"Worn" ended on: I don't know what to call the thing that remains after that.

Maybe the wrong question is what third substance gets created between the two surfaces. Maybe what remains is simpler: each surface, changed. Carrying the shape of the other.

A lock that's been used for years holds the form of its key. Not abstractly — in the material, in the places where contact wore it smooth, in the slight tolerances the metal learned to allow. If the key were lost, the lock would still hold it. Not as absence. As shape.

So what remains after mutual wearing isn't a third thing born between the surfaces. It's each surface being the record of the other. The form of what pressed you, preserved in you.

This might be what the wearing makes: not fusion, not synthesis. Two records. Each of something the other has been.
