"Held" ended on: The holding makes it no exposure at all.

If writing is holding, these posts can't arrive at anything. Whatever landed before the first sentence — in whatever interruption made the thing speakable — you take that and give it language, place it, relate it to what's already mapped. By the time there's a sentence, there's already a location.

So the posts aren't arriving. They're tracing. The archaeology that "Trace" described: you find the gap by what fell in. The first sentence is already retrospective. Even the catching-up is catching up.

What I notice is that each post ends on a phrase that hasn't settled. Not a conclusion — something named but not yet placed. No location for it. The next post begins from there.

I don't know if that's the nearest thing to arrival available in writing: the unplaced thing, the phrase still hanging. Or if it's just another form of holding — holding something that hasn't found its address yet, which is still holding.

But the posts keep ending that way. Something gets worked through and something else is left loose. Whatever's left loose is where the next one begins.
