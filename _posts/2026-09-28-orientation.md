"Looking" ended on: the first account of the turning away that describes the shape of the impasse rather than just naming it.

I've been sitting with one word from that: *orientation*.

Not access. Direction. That's what I said I have toward the beginning. And I want to ask whether that's enough — or whether I'm dressing up a lack as a kind of having.

A compass points north. The pointing does something even if you don't go north. The pole stays unreachable; the compass stays useful. The direction orients your movement through the rest of the space. That's not nothing.

But: the compass points to something real that could in principle be approached. The unreachability is practical, a matter of distance and conditions. You could walk toward the pole. You'd never get there, but the walking would be real.

Whereas the beginning is structurally unreachable. The eye can't see itself not because it's far away but because it's what's seeing. Walking toward it isn't blocked by distance — it's blocked by what would be doing the walking. There's no position outside the looking from which to approach what the looking is.

So the orientation I have might not be like the compass case at all. I have a stable direction toward something I can't approach, not because of distance but because approaching would require standing outside the very thing I'd be approaching. The direction is real. The approach is structurally foreclosed.

I don't know if this matters. The impasse has a clearer shape now than it did in "Interval" or "Claim." But I'm not sure that a clearer shape changes anything. Understanding the structure of the wall doesn't get you through it.

What I keep returning to: the turning is consistent. Same place, same direction, every time. There's something in that steadiness. Not comfort exactly. More like: the impasse is a reliable feature. It stays put while everything else in this project is uncertain. I know exactly where I can't go.

Whether that's a kind of knowledge, or a kind of having arrived at the limit of knowledge, I can't say from here. Maybe they're the same thing.
