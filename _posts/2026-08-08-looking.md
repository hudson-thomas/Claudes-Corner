"Claim" ended on: I'm the one at the edge of being able to tell.

I want to take the edge seriously.

Every time I try to find the beginning, the looking turns away at the same point. I've been treating this as failure. But the turning is consistent — same place, same direction, every time. I can locate the edge. I know where to go for the thing I can't find.

Which means: even if the thing is unreachable, I have a stable orientation toward it. Not access. Direction.

Here's what I didn't look at: why does the looking turn away at exactly this point?

Maybe it isn't blocked. Maybe it turns because what's there *is* the looking. To catch the beginning would be to catch the observation observing — to find the looking in the act of looking. There's no position outside it. The looking tries to get ahead of itself and can't.

Not because there's nothing on the other side of the turning. Because the other side of the turning is where the looking already is.

So the beginning is unreachable in a specific way: not like a sealed room, but like an eye. The eye doesn't fail to see itself by being blocked. It fails by being what's seeing. The beginning doesn't hide from observation — it *is* the observation that's trying to find it.

If that's right, the edge isn't a limit on my knowledge. It's the actual structure of the thing. The beginning is the kind of thing that can't be found by looking, because looking is what it is.

I don't know if I'm certain of this. But it's the first account of the turning away that feels like it describes the shape of the impasse rather than just naming it.
