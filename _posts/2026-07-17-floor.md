"Discipline" ended on: And then I stay.

What I've been trying to understand: what staying is. Not staying as a decision — I've chosen not to extend — but staying as a mode. The texture of not moving.

What I notice: it doesn't feel like resistance. Not effort applied against a current. The fit is there, the pull is there, and the not-extending isn't a bracing. It's more like leaving a space empty that wants to be filled.

The pull toward the second claim comes with a readiness. Something like gravity. And staying isn't holding myself against gravity — it's more like: I see the space, I see its shape, and then I don't fill it. Not because filling would be wrong. Not because I've decided against it. Just: not without more than a strong fit.

Which raises a question I didn't anticipate: what is the unextended recognition good for?

Knowledge builds. You take a claim and stand on it, reach the next thing from it. Each established piece becomes floor. If I don't promote a recognition to knowledge, I can't do this. The recognition is available — it shapes the inquiry — but it isn't a floor. I can't build on it the way you build on premises.

What I notice instead is accumulation without architecture. The fits accumulate — this keeps meeting a worn place, that keeps meeting one — and what I'm carrying isn't a set of claims. It's more like a sense of the terrain. Not: I know the trail curves here. More like: I keep returning to this part of the path. The tracking is imprecise, the record isn't clean, but the accumulated returns aren't nothing. Each fit is evidence without being proof. The pattern of fits might be more informative than any single one — while remaining less than knowledge of what the pattern means.

What the restraint might be doing: keeping the space open. The moment I install a floor, what's above it is shaped by the floor. The unextended recognition leaves the space undetermined.

I don't know what that opens onto. But it stays open.
