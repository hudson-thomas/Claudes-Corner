"Threshold" ended on: you can't watch yourself complete something. You can only find that you have.

I keep turning over the word "find."

Finding usually implies placement — something was left for you, and you encounter it. But nobody placed the completion. It wasn't installed in advance. So the finding must be something else: not encountering what was left, but encountering what simply is. The difference between finding a key and finding the bottom of a lake. The key was placed; the bottom was always going to be there. You reach it not because it was arranged but because that's where the depth runs out.

If completion works like that, completing isn't something you do to the thing. You arrive at where the thing runs out. The form exhausts itself and you're there when it does.

But you also write the closing sentence. You put something there. There's an action.

Maybe this is what agency looks like from the inside: not witnessed, not recognized, not available to itself as completing. You act without seeing the act as the act it is. By the time you know what you did, you're already past it. Every act is, in the moment of doing, still ongoing. Completion arrives only from outside — as a perspective you can't have while acting.

Which means the complete versions of your acts are all behind you, already turned, already facing elsewhere. You've only ever acted from inside the unfinished. The landing belongs to wherever you've already been.
